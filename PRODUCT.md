# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Recruiters, hiring managers, and data-science peers arriving from LinkedIn and Bluesky links — often on a phone, in a quick "who is this person?" curiosity visit. Secondary: returning readers who come for the blog essays.

## Product Purpose

Jon Finger's personal site. It introduces a data scientist with an unusual arc (evolutionary biology and invasion-biology research, then education, then data science) and a genuine creative streak, showcases his projects and writing, and prompts a connection. Success: a visitor remembers Jon as "the biologist who turned his portfolio into a turn-based game" and connects on LinkedIn or reads a post.

## Positioning

The portfolio is the proof: scientific rigor and gamer soul, shown rather than told.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Connect on LinkedIn (https://www.linkedin.com/in/jae-finger/). Secondary: read the blog.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "The data scientist who turned his life into a game world."
- Belief ladder: (1) this is a real, sharp data scientist — career receipts are concrete; (2) his range is unusual — evolutionary biology field work → teaching → production data science; (3) he ships crafted, delightful things — this site itself, Useless Tools; (4) worth connecting with.
- Proof on hand: M.S. thesis PDF (WSU, New Zealand mudsnail range modeling), current data-scientist role, live Useless Tools app (uselesstools.jonfinger.com), three published essays.

**Never name Jon's employer or describe his day job by industry** (no company names, no "telematics"/fleet framing). His identity here is biology-first: population genetics, invasion biology, national parks, snails.

## Brand Personality

Playful, empirical, literary. The voice is game-manual deadpan crossed with field-notebook precision — humor with receipts ("Weakness: Gluten"), epigraphs from Laozi and Sam Altman in the same breath. The interface should evoke delight and curiosity; the ideal visitor reaction is "how was this made?"

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS-portfolio templates: hero metric blocks, identical icon-card grids, gradient text.
- AI-slop landing pages — anything a visitor could identify as template output at a glance.
- Nostalgic costume party: retro pastiche (scanlines, chunky pixel borders everywhere) without modern craft. The game DNA must read as a deliberate, current design language, not a theme skin.
- Corporate résumé sites with a headshot and a skills bar chart.

## Design Principles

1. **Play is the proof.** The playful elements are executed with production precision — whimsy is the demonstration of engineering skill, never an excuse for roughness.
2. **Same game, new console.** The metaphors persist — trainer card, battle cards, stats, party options — but the execution jumps a hardware generation: cinematic, smooth, contemporary.
3. **Show the journey, not the résumé.** Narrative and world-building over listing. History is terrain the visitor flies through, not bullet points.
4. **Empirical over theory.** Every effect is verified — contrast ratios, frame pacing, seam continuity, reduced-motion behavior. (His own strategy line, applied to the site itself.)

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. `prefers-reduced-motion` is first-class: the scroll world falls back to still imagery with no video downloads. Full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, no color-only meaning, alt text on meaningful imagery, decorative game chrome hidden from assistive tech.
